Spell Charges — Part 1

Canon 41 — the substrate. A charge is one banked casting of a working the core doesn't hold. No inventory, no item, no slot, no new UI: it reuses the entire shipped cast surface and adds a single integer per spell id.

1. Why this shape

The backlog framing was "specials / one-shot consumables". That needs an inventory the dungeon side has no concept of, a UI surface to hold it, and a targeting path — three arcs, plus a drift toward direct intervention that the Hand-of-Evil rejection already ruled out.

Charges need none of it. The rule that keeps it honest:

If it cannot be cast at a cell, it is not in this feature. A forged writ that cancels an Inspector dispatch is a good idea, and it belongs to a different arc.

The problem it actually solves is an economy one. Gold has twelve-plus sources and five sinks, and every StockType on the manifest is a permanent grant that IsOwned pulls from stock forever. Nothing in the game could be bought twice. Once the manifest empties, gold stops meaning anything. A charge is the only repeatable purchase shape available — and the only place an effect too strong to sit on a cooldown can live.

2. What part 1 ships (and what it doesn't)

This arc is split in two on purpose. Part 1 is the substrate, testable end-to-end with zero content authored. Part 2 adds the vendors and the twelve items.

3. The rules

Keyed on spell id, never an ordinal. Ids are declared stable and never renamed (canon 38); SpellEffect is append-only. Keying the ledger on an effect ordinal would silently re-key every banked charge in every existing save the first time a working was added.

Charges bypass the affinity type-lock, and pay for it. A Water core can cast a Fire working from a scroll — the permanent roster keeps its lock, so your own signature still means something. What a borrowed god's power loses is reach: an off-affinity cast scales radius and duration by 0.6.

Radius and duration only, never magnitude. Same lever deepening uses, for the reason canon 38 gave: a changed damage number reads as a stat bump and would mean retuning the whole affinity roster twice over. One lever, one rule.

The permanent grant always wins. HeldPermanently is split out from IsAvailable exactly so the consumption point can ask the narrower question. A charge spends only when it's the sole way you hold the working — so researching a spell never quietly eats the scrolls you banked for it.

Nothing is billed for air. Charges spend after SpellCaster.Resolve succeeds, alongside mana and cooldown — the same refusal the arc already makes when a cast finds nothing.

Disagreement is loud. A cast that passes availability with an empty ledger logs a warning rather than granting a free casting. If the two ever disagree, that's a bug worth seeing.

4. Persistence

Two parallel lists on DungeonSaveData:

public List<string> spellChargeIds = new();
public List<int> spellChargeCounts = new();

JsonUtility can't serialise a dictionary, and the paired-list shape already has precedent in the save data. Both additive and empty on legacy saves, so nothing migrates — an old save loads with an empty ledger and no special-casing.

A length mismatch (only reachable by hand-editing a save) takes the shorter of the two rather than throwing. A corrupt ledger must not cost the player the rest of the file.

The static ledger clears on scene wake alongside the cooldowns; the load path repopulates it immediately afterwards.

6. What part 2 will need from you

Nothing yet — but two authoring decisions are already locked and worth knowing:

A thrall MonsterDefinition for Kethra's Undying Coal, authored fresh rather than reusing the necromancer's raised minion, so it doesn't inherit balance meant for something else. That's a sprite slot that will want art eventually.

Given workings need unlock keys nothing grants. That's what makes them unlearnable: HeldPermanently returns false forever, so charges are the only access. No new flag needed — it falls out of the existing schema.

Three items changed shape during design and the reasons are worth keeping:

  • The Weight Below (seal a corridor) was dropped. CanBuildWallAt ends with AnyEntityStandsIn and refuses — canon 36's answer to burial is that it never lets one happen. An area seal would always target occupied cells. Morrun gets floor-wide BoonArmour instead.
  • The Undying Coal was going to drive spawner respawns, but TickRespawn refuses while any adventurer walks the floor — exactly when you'd cast it. Your thrall framing dissolved it: SpawnTransientMinion is public, holds no capacity, self-destructs, and is explicitly skipped by the save.
  • The Drowned Mouth went room-scale, not floor-scale. Pull's force is clamped to secondary and KnockbackStep stops dead at walls, so floor-scale would have been a metre of shuffling. Inside one chamber there's nothing between the bodies and the mark.
