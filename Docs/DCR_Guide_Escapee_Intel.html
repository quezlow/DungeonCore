Escapee Intel — Trap-Wise Returns

Canon 48 — the survivors carry home one more fact, and it costs you a rogue.

1. What shipped, and why it is small

Backlog D7, delivered as an extension of the grudge pipeline rather than a system of its own. A survivor already carries one fact home — grudgeMonster rides TrackedMember and returns as returnGrudge, biasing target choice. This adds a second fact at the party level: whether the architecture or the garrison did the killing. When traps account for at least half of a tracked party's actual deaths, the record is marked trapWise, and the return seats a Rogue in one of the fresh slots — the class whose canDetectTraps flags and disarms are already shipped teeth.

Decay costs nothing. TakeReturningParty destroys the record and the return's own resolution writes a fresh one, so the intel recomputes from the new visit's deaths. Retire the trap corridor and the next record carries nothing. Rotating pressure, zero standing state, no timer.

Survivors are untouched. They return as their exact selves per the entry-4 promise; the seat only ever takes a replacement slot, and it is skipped when a surviving member already is a Rogue.

Adjacent open item worth knowing about: the Phase 9 polish trio includes a per-trap fire counter. Once that ships, the two read together — the counter shows which corridor taught them. Nothing here depends on it.
2. How it works — the pipeline

Tallies. Traps were invisible to the damage ledger: every contact trap dealt bare TakeDamage(dmg) (the Melee default the code itself files under “environmental sources”), and the crossbow's bolt shipped sourceName = "". Two per-body totals close that: trapDamageTaken and monsterDamageTaken (fed from RecordDamagedBy, so the monster side is the sum the grudge argmax already walks). The eight contact traps now call TakeTrapDamage — record, then wound, in that order because a killing blow runs Die() from inside TakeDamage. The crossbow records through a fromTrap payload flag and keeps its Ranged kind, so the shield wall still mitigates bolts. Both ledgers record listed damage pre-mitigation, matching the grudge precedent.

Verdict. At death, diedToTraps = trap total beats monster total. At resolution, TrackedParty.trapWise = trap deaths are at least half the actual deaths, minimum one. Captures and core-breaches sit outside the ratio — a member taken alive is not a trap story — which needed one new flag, PartyMember.captured, set in TryCapture, because a capture and a death were indistinguishable on the record.

The seat. In SpawnReturningParty, the first fallen-replacement whose type is a combatant (the same goal test as ResolveCombatClass: BreachCore / SeekDeath / LootAndLeave) spawns with forcedClass = ClassDefFor(CombatClass.Rogue). A pinned pilgrim party can never be handed a sword. If no Rogue definition is listed on the spawner, the seat falls back to an ordinary roll and the alert stays quiet rather than lying.

Persistence, all additive. trapWise on TrackedParty; diedToTraps + captured on the roster half of LiveMemberSaveData (a resolved-dead member persists through AddResolvedMember, so a mid-raid save/load would otherwise launder the cause); the two tallies on the live half beside grudgeDamage. Legacy saves read false and zero; no migration.

3. Verify — smoke steps
A capture is not a trap death. If the corridor feeds a Capture Trap and a prison cell, taken-alive members sit outside the ratio on both sides — expected, not a fault.
