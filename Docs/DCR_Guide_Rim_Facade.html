1 · First look, before tuning anything
If the count of still-fogged cells is not zero, the reveal did not run — check that floor 0's DungeonTerrain resolved its FloorRoot. If the ring is revealed but bare, the wall set never got it; that is the renderer half, not the terrain half. The diagnostic separates the two on purpose.

2 · The bedrock family

Without a family the ring renders the ordinary stone path at bedrock's slate stoneTint (0.32, 0.33, 0.4). That is a legitimate shipping state, and it is worth seeing before you draw anything.

Bedrock resists at 9999 and is never made claimable, so the facade can never be mined through. That is existing behaviour, not something this arc added — but it is why the wall is permanent and why no gate needs guarding.

b. Rim gloom — on the SurfaceZoneGenerator

KnobDefaultWhat it does
rimGloomCells8How far out the shading reaches. 0 disables.
rimGloomMaxAlpha0.45Darkness at the wall itself.
rimGloomFalloff21 = linear; higher hugs the wall and avoids a second edge where the gloom ends.
rimGloomColorcool shadowSeparate from the edge fog colour, which is a pale mist for the far rim.
It paints on the surface fog tilemap, which the scene puts on Player order 100: over the grass and over the wall's draped face — a contact shadow at its foot — but under the caps and under the dungeon's own fog. If you want the face left clean, lower rimGloomMaxAlpha; the layering is not adjustable without a sorting change, and Appendix B is emphatic about those.

c. The rubble ring — on the SurfaceZoneProfile

This is probably the biggest single win. A perfect circle of empty grass hugging the wall reads as machine-made more than the brightness step does, and rubble breaks the silhouette the way a cliff foot actually looks.
