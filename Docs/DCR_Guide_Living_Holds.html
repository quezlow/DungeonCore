The Living Holds

Canon 19, build order step 7 — walking villagers, road patrols, the multi-day caravan and its one verb, the held-stretch toll, and the entry-7 matrix made real.

1. What shipped, in one breath

The deep roads have people on them. The village's dwarves now walk short lane hops inside their own hold and stand still at night. A patrol paces a bounded beat through the gatehouse and two more wander the village floor's whole network, walking broken roads to where they stop, pausing to look at the collapse, and turning back. A caravan crosses between gatehouse and village on a multi-day schedule — a real journey with a camp at dusk, an unseen transit between floors, and a day's trading at the village before the road home.

The player gets exactly one verb per caravan: click the wagon and Rob it (all the cargo, standing -25, the road goes quiet for extra days), Tax it (only while it stands on a road stretch you hold — every cell claimed — 20% of the original cargo, standing -3), or Let it pass. The first held crossing ever plays a scripted camera beat: the view glides to the wagon under input lock and the wisp explains the choice. Every later crossing is one alert; the panel only ever opens from a manual click.

And the entry-7 matrix finally holds a real edge: Dwarves <-> Holy Order is Hostile, and it is exercised — patrols withdraw toward home when the Church's people come near, and caravans hurry past them.

3. The road is the route: DeepRoadGraph

MarkNaturalFloor put every road cell in minedTiles, so the pathfinder would happily carry a dwarven caravan through the player's own tunnels whenever they were shorter — exactly wrong. So nothing here pathfinds. Walkers follow the road's own centreline cells, which is what keeps them visibly on the carriageway, threads them through the village gates the site plans drew (the carriageway was subtracted from the sites, never the reverse), and wades them across river washouts.

DeepRoadGraph is pure static, like RoadNetworkBuilder, and for the same reason: the route report in Commands.cs must measure a route without play mode. It rebuilds the network as a graph from saved RoadData: rails (walkable centrelines), nodes (endpoint clusters merged at radius 6 — the exact rule RebuildRoadAnchors ships), and BFS routing with Bresenham stitches across junction mouths.

Rim ends are where a road leaves for another floor, in fiction. Identified from save data alone: kind == Trunk and the RAW polyline end within 2 cells of clampRadius, which is where OnCircle put it at generation. The broken gap never moves the raw endpoint, so this holds for floor index 3's collapsed rim trunks exactly as for floor index 2's unbroken one; spurs are RoadKind.Spur and never qualify. The caravan pairs one rim end per floor by bearing, so the wagon visibly leaves and arrives on "the same" road.

4. Villagers walk

DwarvenVillageController keeps its whole discovery contract — poll, establish, name roster, alert, wisp lines, click greeting — and swaps bare SpriteRenderers for DwarfWalkerPuppets. Each villager waits 4–10 seconds, hops to a lane cell within 6 cells, and waits again. Hops are found by a bounded BFS inside the site's own cells (200 expansions, 4-neighbour), so nobody ever strays onto the carriageway or into the rock: the candidate rule that placed them — two carved north neighbours — still picks every destination.

Night stills the lanes: villagers freeze at dusk and resume at dawn, the same clock the caravan camps by. villagerCount now defaults to 8 — the knob the arc was always going to grow. Walk speed 1.2, deliberately slower than any adventurer: these are people at home, not units.

5. Patrols

DwarvenPatrolController is a scene singleton and stateless on purpose — patrols are ambient texture and re-derive each session; persisting a guard's footsteps would be save weight spent on nothing a player could notice.

Gatehouse: once the outpost is established, one patrol paces a bounded beat — 60 centreline cells either side of the outpost — through the gate, day and night. Village floor: once the village is established, two patrols set off from it in opposite directions and wander the whole network, picking a random connected road at each junction (no immediate about-face when the junction offers anywhere else to go). At a broken end — a spur's collapse or a rim trunk's swallowed end — the patrol walks to where the road stops, pauses three seconds facing past the collapse along the road's own bearing, and turns back. That is rung 5 of canon 19's warning ladder rehearsed on natural geometry; step 8 re-aims a beat that already works instead of inventing one.

Reactions exercise the matrix (chapter 8): any adventurer within 8 units halts the patrol to watch, facing the nearest; an adventurer of a Hostile faction sends it withdrawing toward home until nothing hostile is within 14. Scans are throttled to twice a second — the ScanForHostiles allocation lesson, not repeated.

Patrol speed is a plain 2.2, not day-derived: a loop has no arrival to keep, so the days constraint does not bind it. The first time a patrol is seen on a revealed stretch, the wisp speaks patrol_first (once ever, wisp-persisted).

6. The caravan: a journey in days

The journey is two legs and a gap, because the gatehouse and the village are different floors and no walker crosses floors: outpost to rim end on the gatehouse floor (0.75 days), an unseen transit through the rock (1 day), rim trunk down the network to the village (1.5 days), a dwell at the market (1 day), and the same road home. One caravan at a time; the next departs 2–4 days after a journey completes (+4 extra after a robbery). Departures need the Deep Holds met (outpost or village established), both dwarven floors generated, and Tier 0 standing — at Tier 1+ the road goes quiet while the gate still trades, which is the way back.

Travel is authored in days, never tiles per second — the canon constraint, kept: each leg's speed is derived as route length over authored days times DayDuration, so lengthening the day lengthens the crossing instead of silently halving it. Walking happens in the day phase only; the wagon visibly camps at dusk, which is what makes a multi-day presence legible. Transit and dwell elapse in calendar time (day plus night) — nothing is on screen to camp.

One refinement to the persistence fork, flagged: the locked wording was "position is a pure function of the clock". Built literally against the wall clock, every halt — night, the vignette, an open panel — would end in a teleport catch-up. What ships preserves the intent exactly: the save carries walked seconds this leg (accrued only while actually walking) and phase seconds, and position is a pure function of those. Reload restores the wagon to the cell it stood on; halts simply do not accrue; no per-frame progress field exists to drift.

Cargo rolls 80–200g per journey. The column is three walkers plus the optional cart, spaced 1.6 units along the path. While a Hostile-faction adventurer is within 10 units, the wagon hurries at 1.5x — fear on the road, the matrix felt.

Sighting: fog hides walkers on unrevealed road for free (the shadow tilemap renders above the Player layer). The first time ever the wagon rolls onto a stretch the player has revealed: one Discovery alert, caravan_first, and FactionIntel.NotifyEncounter — a caravan can genuinely be the player's first meeting with the Holds.

7. The one verb, and the toll

Click the wagon (once sighted) and the CaravanActionPanel opens; the wagon halts while the choice is open. One verb per caravan: Rob, Tax, or Let pass settles the wagon for its whole journey — Let pass consumes the verb too. Closing without choosing settles nothing: the wagon walks on and can be clicked later; a misclick must never burn the decision.

VerbTakesStandingThen
Roball cargo-25survivors flee to the nearest refuge (their site, or the rim if closer) at hurry pace, day and night; next departure +4 extra days; at Tier 1+ the road falls quiet
Tax20% of original cargo, min 1-3wagon walks on, lighter
Let passnothing—wagon walks on; the verb is spent

Tax needs a held road: the button is live only while the wagon stands on a segment where every carriageway cell is influence-claimed — the new TerrainFeatureGenerator.IsRoadSegmentHeld, placed on the generator (not the caravan) because step 8's claiming penalties will key on the same test. Claiming itself carries no penalty yet; that is step 8.

The first held crossing ever plays the toll vignette: the camera glides to the wagon under input lock (the First Blood pattern — follow target, zoom 7, scaled waits so pausing holds the beat), the wisp gives the spiel — "...take it all and be a robber, take a toll and be a power, or wave it through and be forgotten. One choice per wagon." — and the wagon holds a few grace seconds inviting the click. The beat is flag-persisted and never replays. Every later crossing of a held stretch is one System alert per segment per journey while the verb is unspent, and nothing more — the panel only ever opens from a manual click. That is the anti-spam decision of the arc.

8. The matrix revisit (entry 7)

FactionRelations.Between now writes the Deep Holds' first edge: Dwarves <-> Holy Order is Hostile. The deep-faith reading — divinity below, the Church's burning judgment above — earned its way into the matrix the day a shipped system could exercise it, and two now do: patrol withdrawal and caravan hurry. Deliberately not Allied to the Cultists: sharing an enemy of the Church is not sharing a cause with people who worship what the Holds merely live beside. Everything else stays Neutral; strengths stay 8 across the board.

9. Save and load

Eight flat additive fields on DungeonSaveData: caravanNextDepartureDay (-1 = due), caravanState, caravanWalkedSeconds, caravanPhaseSeconds, caravanCargo, caravanVerbUsed, caravanSighted, caravanTollVignettePlayed. Old saves load the lot as zero/false/−1 and the caravan simply departs on the first eligible day. The journey state enum serialises as ints — append only, never reorder.

On load, routes are not in the save: they re-derive deterministically from the world (same floors, same graph, same bearing pairing), the current leg re-stages, and position falls out of the saved walking clock. Patrols persist nothing and respawn from the establish flags. The vignette flag and the sighting flag are static, merchant-pattern, and reset with ResetForNewGame.
