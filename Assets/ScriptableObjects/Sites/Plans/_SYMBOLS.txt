SITE PLAN SYMBOLS -- quick reference
====================================

Not a plan. Plans are an explicit TextAsset list on AncientSiteProfile, not a
folder scan, so this file is never parsed. Keep it here anyway: this is where
you are when you need it.

The full explanation lives in Docs/DCR_Guide_Content_Authoring.html.
Canon is Docs/DESIGN_CANON.md and outranks both.


GLYPHS
------

  #   MASONRY. Solid, mineable, retyped to the site's family terrain.

  .   CARVED FLOOR. Open ground. Opened by MarkNaturalFloor on REVEAL, which
      bypasses the mine path -- which is why mining a site never bills anything
      except its heart.

  X   THE HEART. Exactly one per plan; two is a parse error. Masonry AND the
      one cell carrying the site's meaning -- altar, grave slab, capped font,
      seal-stone. Solid, because unsealing it means mining it.

      DO NOT put the heart on the plan's centre if the plan anchors AlongRoad.
      The centre lands on the carriageway, the road subtraction takes the cell,
      and the seal becomes unbreakable. The validator now fails this.

  =   PLATFORM. Floor, raised. Real: it carries a Rigidbody2D collider.

  ^   STAIRS. Floor, and the only opening in a platform's edge. At least one
      per platform, and a stair run is three cells wide.

  +   DOOR. Floor plus a marker. THREE CELLS in its run, never two.
      Declared, never inferred -- a two-cell gap between grave slabs is
      structurally identical to a two-cell doorway, and 815 furniture gaps
      across the shipped plans prove nothing can tell them apart. Only '+'
      cells are checked, so stall gaps and grave rows are exempt.

  ~   LANE. Floor plus a marker: where a road may run THROUGH the site, door
      to door. A REGION, not a line -- paint the ground a road may use and let
      obstacles stand in it. The lane parts around a font or a plinth on its
      own; you only draw a separate branch to reach a THIRD door.

      Width is not a fixed rule. Through MASONRY a lane needs three cells,
      because a floor cell is walkable only when y+1 AND y+2 are also floor --
      so one and two wide are buried under the drape. Across OPEN floor a
      narrow lane is fine, since the open ground supplies the clearance. The
      validator does not measure width: it walks the lane and tells you whether
      a road could.

      Match the gate. A five-wide door feeding a three-wide lane pinches.

      No lane means no through-route: a road reaches the door and stops. That
      is what the sealed vaults want.

  ' ' SPACE -- not part of the site at all. LOAD-BEARING, not a fallthrough:
      BrokenAqueduct_TheDrySpan is two separate reaches with a void between
      them where the middle span fell. Do not tighten this into an error.

  Anything else is ignored the same way as space.


HEADERS
-------

  @archetype:    Required. A SiteArchetype name.
  @name:         Required. Used by the decor registry and the guarantee pins.

  @anchor:       Free | AlongRoad | Junction | Crossing | RoadEnd
                 Where the plan wants to sit. Falls back to a free pick when
                 the preference cannot be met -- see @anchor_required.

  @anchor_required: yes | no   (default no)
                 Refuse the fallback. A plan whose meaning IS its position --
                 a chapel on the road a sealing was administered from -- is
                 better skipped than stranded in open rock. Skipped placements
                 are reported by name and reason in the site report.

  @anchor_on:    door | centre   (default centre)
                 Land a declared DOOR on the anchor instead of the bounding-box
                 centre, and reject anchors the door does not face. Roads then
                 arrive AT the entrance rather than at whatever wall the bearing
                 happened to cross.

  @rotate:       yes | no   (default yes)
                 Four quarter turns and a mirror. Say WHY when you turn it off;
                 two plans currently carry no reason and one carries a circular
                 one, and a constraint nobody can explain gets removed by
                 somebody eventually.

  @doors:        unmarked | none | marked   REQUIRED.
                 unmarked -- not yet annotated. Passes, and the report keeps a
                            roll-call of them.
                 none     -- genuinely has no doors. Passes silently.
                 marked   -- doors are drawn with '+', rule enforced.
                 A MISSING header fails. Omission is not a third answer.

  @general:      yes | no   (default yes)
                 'no' keeps the plan out of the general pool, reserving it for
                 a guarantee pass.


THE RULES THAT CATCH PEOPLE OUT
-------------------------------

THE DRAPE. A floor cell is walkable only when the cells at y+1 and y+2 are
also floor. Walls are drawn draping downward, and a cell with less than two
clear above it is buried. Everything below follows from this one fact.

  - A two-cell gap seals. Three does not. Wall thickness is irrelevant.
  - A one-wide east-west corridor has NO walkable cells.
  - A one-wide north-south corridor works until the plan rotates, which is
    worse than failing outright -- correct in the orientation it was drawn in.
  - The two cells directly below any solid cell are unwalkable. A road always
    parts around a centred font; nothing passes beneath it at any width.

A WALL RECESS IS THREE DEEP. Two leaves a pinched run that seals under the
drape.

HEART CLEARANCE IS MEASURED AROUND THE HEART'S SOLID COMPONENT, not the heart
cell. Measuring from the cell failed six of eight plans, because a plinth-set
heart has masonry neighbours by design.

CONNECTIVITY GATES NOTHING. A site is MINED into, not walked into, so a
disconnected chamber is a room the player opens with a pick.
SunkenPlaza_TheCountingFloor is seven pieces and entirely correct. The figure
is reported because it is informative, not because it is a rule.
